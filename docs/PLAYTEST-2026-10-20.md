# Lab playtest, 2026-10-20

The second playtest, after the day's eight PRs (#57 to #64). Three Sonnet
agents played all ten Lab games on localhost at phone width. Each game got
daily and random rounds, with some answers wrong on purpose, plus an audit
of the data. Full agent notes were kept in the session scratchpad. Findings
marked "checked" were confirmed against the data; the rest are the agents'
reports.

Fun is out of 5, higher is better. Every game was playable within about 2 s
on a warm dev server (Clue ladder and Who am I slowest, at 1.8 s).

## What today's changes did

- Missing link: clue 1 rules out all three wrong options in 8% of geography rounds (was 49%) and 1% of film rounds (was 15%).
- Odd one out: 0 of 563 geography odd ones on another continent (was 78%).
- Scramble: ramp works (average letters by round 4.7 up to 10.8); fact line after every round.
- Twin flags: all 68 sentences checked against the rendered flags; 0 back-to-back repeats in 5,000 games.
- Timeline: "Same year:" line works.
- Four by four: all 19 hidden-word groups and every trick fair; 11 boards solved.

## Errors found, fixed the same day

- Who am I: works clues used Wikidata's best-rank statements only, so a
  person with one preferred-rank club kept just that club (Robert
  Lewandowski "Played for Chicago Fire"). Now every non-deprecated
  statement. A second bug: the description "National Basketball
  Association team" matched the national-team filter, so Stephen Curry
  "Played for Davidson". College sides are now skipped, national sides
  matched on their name. Kyle Kuzma's Guangdong credit is wrong in Wikidata
  and is excluded; with no other team listed he leaves the pool.
- Scramble facts: China "borders 16 countries" counted Hong Kong and Macau;
  Sri Lanka "borders India" is one-way in countries.json. Only mutual
  borders with countries count now.
- Scramble hint: "Capital of Switzerland" handed over Bern. Now "A capital in
  Western Europe".
- Clue ladder: "The month of August is named after him" now masks August.
- Twin flags results: "Ecuador: Ecuador has..." now "Ecuador or Colombia.
  Ecuador has...".

Since: Name the year hard mode now gives a second try after a guess 5 or
more years out ("Later than 1950"), for half points, and every miss names
something from the year guessed. Timeline's 2023 Emmy card says the awards
were held in January 2024. Odd one out's region rounds leave out 23
countries, and their capitals, that the UN places where many would not
(Iran, Harare, Hungary): 230 of 1,270 region rounds in 2,000 games had
turned on one. Who am I's works clue names works the person's Wikipedia
opening mentions first (Laura Prepon: That '70s Show and Orange Is the New
Black, not House M.D.); 1,058 of 2,960 clues changed.
Four by four's traps use only tiles that are what the end screen calls them
(not Taylor as a job, Crowe or Bird as birds); 5 puzzles rebuilt. And 30
puzzles where one field filled 3 or 4 of the 4 groups (all music, all
"Famous X") swap one group, mostly for geography or film: puzzles with 3 or more
groups from one field went from 39 to 11, the 6 all-names ones left being
traps of surnames.
Who am I gives no works clue where the person's Wikipedia opening names
none of the works (Karen Carpenter "Recorded Looking for Love"): 301 of
2,960 people are no longer an answer, and no clue names a slur. Paul Thomas
Anderson and Sam Elliott were already right after the opening-first change.
Obscure-est had two Four Tops: the charts credit "Four Tops" in the 1960s
and "The Four Tops" in the 1970s, so Reach Out I'll Be There came in as a
deep cut and Baby I Need Your Loving was missing. Credits now merge without
"The" (the Black Eyed Peas list gained a song, and two Bee Gees and
Black Eyed Peas hits moved up).
Odd one out loads a file of only the answers it can ask (49 KB gzipped, not
the 333 KB obscure.json), with the same rounds: 2.05 s to its first screen on
a slow phone, down from 5.21 s. Google's sign-in script (100 KB) now loads
only when the account menu opens, and the default look's font drops its
optical-size axis (129 KB to 78 KB): Who am I 4.66 s to 3.65 s, Clue ladder
5.16 s to 4.36 s.
Who am I counts a notable work of the right kind as backing when the
Wikipedia opening names none: Jacques Brel is "Recorded Ne me quitte pas and
Amsterdam" again, not "Starred in Far West". 84 of 17,445 clues changed
(about 40 better, 13 weaker, by eye), 16 people gained one, none lost one.
