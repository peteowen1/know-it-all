# Lab playtest, 2026-10-19

Three Sonnet agents played all ten Lab games on localhost (daily and random
rounds, some answers wrong on purpose) and sampled about 50 rounds per game from
the data. Findings below are the agents' reports, not yet checked against the code.
Fun is out of 5, higher is better. Every game loaded in about 1 s or less.

| Game | Fun | Main problem |
|---|---|---|
| Name the year | 4 | Hard mode scores 0 beyond 4 years out |
| Clue ladder | 4 | "Which of these…" clue before options show; Australia-heavy |
| Four by four | 3.5 | Same shape every day: 2 "Hits by", a "Famous X", one other |
| Timeline | 3.5 | Hard mode is a coin flip; wrong order not explained |
| Obscure-est | 3.5 | Rejects "St Lucia", "The Gambia", "Dr Zhivago"; score reason hidden |
| Missing link | 3 | Geo and film guessable at clue 1; people rounds pure recall |
| Odd one out | 3 | Geo and film odd one far too obvious |
| Twin flags | 3 | Only 42 pairs; same country in back-to-back rounds |
| Scramble | 3 | Too easy (19-20/20); wrong guess says only "Not quite" |
| Who am I | 2.5 | Clue 1 "American actor", clue 2 a birth year, clue 3 the shared first name |

## Four by four variety (all 243 puzzles, 972 groups)

- 29% "Hits by [artist]", 25% "Famous [first name]s", 14% "Directed by", 9% borders/capitals.
- 182 of 243 puzzles have a "Famous X" group; 101 have two or more "Hits by" groups.
- Wordplay is about 10% (22 group types) and reuses the same four items: "Hits with a month in the title" is identical in #61, #89, #240.
- No phrase groups, hidden words, homophones or designed red herrings.
- 0 tiles fit two groups (the build check holds).

## Wrong answers teach little

Only Odd one out ("Yeah! is a hit song by Ludacris"), Clue ladder's reveal and
Name the year's end screen explain anything. Four by four's loss screen, Missing
link, Scramble ("It was Gabon."), Timeline and Who am I show only the answer.

## Data to check

- Obscure-est: duplicate "The Weeknd" / "the Weeknd" categories; "You Right" rated more obvious than "Blinding Lights"; doubtful credits (The Physician for David Lean, Man's Fate for Zinnemann, Teach Me! for Nichols); "Abidjan" not accepted.
- Missing link: Billie Eilish tour film credited to James Cameron; "Lee: Gi-kwang" name fragment.
- Timeline / Name the year: Oscar cards dated by film year (Crash 2005, won March 2006); 2023 Emmy held January 2024.
- Clue ladder: "Queen ▢▢▢" hides Victoria in Great Victoria Desert.
- Unconfirmed 2025-26 facts: Andy Burnham PM 2026, Widow's Bay Emmy 2026, Italy 62 vs China 61 UNESCO sites.

## Ideas

- Four by four: phrase groups (tiles form a known phrase, label is not the phrase), hidden words, homophones, deliberate decoys, bigger pools for wordplay groups.
- A one-line "why" on every miss across all games.
- Who am I: a famous-for fact as clue 1, a pageview floor, drop the first-name clue.
- Twin flags: more pairs, no repeat country in consecutive rounds, bigger flags.
- Scramble: a fact on reveal, longer words as the game goes on.
